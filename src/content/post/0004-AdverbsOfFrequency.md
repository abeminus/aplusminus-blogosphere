Adverbs of frequency.

There was always eucalyptus and sage. When the sun was low in the sky, the sound of the waves disappeared. The water dried salty on our skin. We were both yellow and orange and young. I said we should probably go. She asked why, when we had all the time in the world. The wind carried the air from the mountains to the beach. There was always eucalyptus and sage.

Sometimes American flags waved on porches. The sun came through the trees on the other side of the block. Running and laughing we explored and pretended. The voices of our friends grew louder. The dirt ground into our clothes all brown. We laughed and fought in English and Spanish. The wind blew our hair into funny shapes of blonde and brown. Sometimes American flags waved on porches.

Often there was arguing in the evenings. We avoided home after school to go skateboarding or surfing. The week’s homework piled up in our bags all serious. We were young and angry and full of energy. The beach was less than a mile from home and even closer to the high school. She wondered out loud if we should go home soon. I asked her why, the beach is way better. The sound of the waves was nice. Often there was arguing in the evenings.

Seldom did it rain. The rain always came in the fall in southern California. When it did, the smell of eucalyptus grew stronger. The streets would heat up all steamy with the smell of asphalt. We took for granted the walks to school on rainy days. The sounds of water dripping off the sycamores and maples and jacarandas and figs mixed with our footsteps. We were in no hurry. School would always be there and I learned more than I needed on the way. Seldom did it rain.

Serious thoughts about the future never surfaced. Our neurons fired constantly. Our hearts pumped oxygen to our brains all furious. The trails in the mountains were lined with sage and licorice-smelling fennel. I asked her if we should keep going. She said of course, there’s so many red tails in the sky, can’t you hear their cries? We spoke to each other only in the present tense. We had all the time in the world. Serious thoughts about the future never surfaced.

Rarely did the stars come out in June. We ran to our cars and headed to the beach after school. The fog rolled in all thick and quiet. The sound of the waves grew softer. We lit a fire so we could see each other’s faces. The sand beneath our feet felt cold and rough. We talked to each other in English and Spanish. I asked her if she would stay. She said she was going to college in the fall. Rarely did the stars come out in June.

Occasionally the light would shine through her hair. We sat on the sand by the concrete wall under the cypress tree and talked all night. The breeze blew her hair into her eyes all quiet and warm. We tried our best to convince ourselves that we still had time. Her skin smelled like salt and surfboard wax. She asked me if I would remember. I said of course, there are so many things that will remind me. Occasionally I think about the light shining through her hair.

I am so far away now. I live nowhere near any eucalyptus trees.
